## Task 2 — Is §7 actually an open problem?

### 2.1 Known results in queueing with branching service

Under the stated assumptions — work-conserving FIFO service, no abandonment, each root's closure a Galton–Watson tree with offspring mean μ, arrivals at rate λ_G, acts at rate λ_V — the system is a **queue with branching-type service**. This is not an open problem; it reduces to standard models:

- **Crump–Mode–Jagers (CMJ) branching processes** generalize Galton–Watson by allowing individuals to produce offspring over their lifetimes. The total progeny and extinction results are classical (Jagers 1975, *Branching Processes with Biological Applications*; Mode 1971, *Multitype Branching Processes*).
- **Queues with feedback / batch service:** The M/G/1 queue with feedback (where service completion may generate new customers) has been studied since the 1960s. The stability condition for such systems typically takes the form ρ < 1 where ρ is the *effective* load including feedback. If each service act generates on average μ new acts, the effective service requirement per root is 1/(1−μ) acts, and the stability condition is λ_G · E[closure size] < λ_V, i.e. λ_G/(1−μ) < λ_V, or equivalently λ_G < (1−μ)λ_V. This is standard for work-conserving single-server queues with branching service (see e.g. Disney & König 1985, "Queueing Networks: A Survey of their Random Processes", §5 on networks with feedback; or Takagi 1991, *Queueing Analysis*, Vol. 1, Chapter 4 on M/G/1 with feedback).
- **Processor sharing with branching:** The v0.1 counterexamples (short-closure-first scheduler beats the mean-size bound) are exactly the reason queueing theory distinguishes *work-conserving FIFO* from *size-aware* scheduling. Under FIFO with no size information, the mean-load bound is tight; under size-aware scheduling (SRPT, shortest-remaining-processing-time), it is not. The v0.1 closed forms failed because they assumed mean-size accounting without specifying the scheduler.

**Conclusion:** Under the exact assumptions the v0.2 draft names (work-conserving FIFO, no abandonment, GW closure), the stability condition λ_G < (1−μ)λ_V is **likely a standard result** in the branching-service queueing literature, not a novel open problem. The honest framing is "reduces to a known form under assumptions A, and the counterexamples above show it fails under B" (which the v0.2 draft §7 already says). What is *genuinely* open is whether the Monday-corpus process satisfies those assumptions (offspring are not i.i.d., not depth-independent, and the selection is purposive, not random).

### 2.2 The `g` inconsistency and minimal well-posed model

**Inconsistency:** v0.1 used `g` as both a "closure fraction" in prose and as `g = λ_G/λ_V` in the numeric counterexample. The v0.2 draft §7 flags this but does not fix it.

**Minimal well-posed model the paper should state:**

> Consider a single-server FIFO queue with infinite buffer. Claims arrive as a Poisson process at rate λ_G. Each claim, upon entering service, initiates a verification act that takes Exp(λ_V) time. Upon act completion, the claim produces N new claims that join the back of the same FIFO queue, where N ~ offspring distribution with mean μ. The original claim is *closed* only when its entire Galton–Watson progeny tree is processed. The system is stable iff the effective load ρ = λ_G / ((1−μ)λ_V) < 1, i.e. λ_G < (1−μ)λ_V. At μ ≥ 1, the expected closure size is infinite and no finite service rate stabilizes the backlog.

**What the current text must fix:**
1. Define the queue state (is it the number of *roots* waiting, or the total number of *acts* including offspring?).
2. State when offspring join the queue (immediately upon generation, or batched?).
3. Specify service times (are acts i.i.d. Exp(λ_V) or general?).
4. Fix the scheduler (FIFO, work-conserving, no size information).
5. State the stability proposition exactly (in terms of which process — backlog, unfinished work, number of roots).
6. Clarify that μ ≥ 1 with infinite-mean closure is a *transience* result, not a throughput→0 result (throughput→0 requires the scheduler to be unable to prioritize short closures).
7. Add the expiry/reflection correction: if claims expire at rate δ, the reflected queue equilibrates at max(0, (λ_G − (1−μ)λ_V)/δ).

**Finding:** `scope_boundary` — §7 is not an open problem under its own stated assumptions; it reduces to known branching-service queueing. The paper should cite CMJ/M/G/1-with-feedback literature rather than frame it as novel (`paper-v0.2-draft.md` §7 at `<25123df>`).

## Task 3 — Prior-art search log for paper B (`addressing-is-equality`)

### 3.1 Search log

| Candidate | Anticipates the claimed sliver? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **NbE freshness side condition** | **Only a component.** The §3.1 marker collision (Church zero vs λf.λx.X) is structurally the classical NbE/readback freshness condition — residual markers must be fresh / unnameable (de Bruijn levels, gensym). The v0.2 draft §6 acknowledges this: "the §3.1 marker collision is, structurally, the classical NbE/readback freshness condition." The paper's "admitted domain that refuses terms naming the marker" is that same side-condition under another name. It does **not** anticipate the *priced settlement with receipt* composition. | Berger & Schwichtenberg 1991; Abel 2013 habilitation; PLS Lab NbE survey. |
| **Unison** | **Only a component.** Unison makes the content hash the definition's identity — "the hash of a term or type is its true name." This is content-addressed identity of *code*, not of *computed normal-form equality with a budgeted receipt*. | unison-lang.org docs; Medium 2026-02-26 article. |
| **Dhall semantic integrity hashes** | **Only a component.** Dhall's integrity check is literally the hash of a normalized expression: "the hash is the true address and the file path is just a suggestion." This is content-addressed identity of *normal forms*, but without the priced evaluation, the two-sided comparison, or the ATP spend as part of the verdict. | Dhall.Tutorial (Hackage); Haskellforall 2017-11-03. |
| **Nix derivation hashes** | **Only a component.** Nix has fixed-output derivations (content-addressed by specified hash) and floating CA derivations (content-addressed by computed output hash). The hash is the identity of the *build product*, not a runtime equality verdict with a receipt. | Nix RFC 0062 (2019-08-14); Nix Reference Manual; NixOS Wiki ca-derivations. |
| **IPLD / Merkle identity** | **Only a component.** IPLD is "software for querying/traversing across any Merkle DAG"; Merkle DAGs give content-addressed identity of data structures. No pricing, no equality settlement, no receipt. | IPLD docs; IPFS Merkle DAG docs; IPLD Glossary. |
| **Hash-consing (Ershov; Filliâtre–Conchon)** | **Only a component.** Hash-consing gives O(1) structural equality of already-constructed terms by sharing values that are structurally equal. The modern statement is Filliâtre & Conchon 2006, "Type-Safe Modular Hash-Consing". This is the *address-sharing mechanism*, not semantic equality of computed values with a priced receipt. | Ershov 1958 (CACM); Filliâtre & Conchon 2006 (ACM ML Workshop); Wikipedia Hash consing. |
| **Gas-metered VMs (Ethereum)** | **Only a component.** Ethereum's EVM meters every operation in gas, produces a transaction receipt with gasUsed, and the receipt is part of the consensus state. This is *priced execution with a receipt*, but the receipt attests to *execution*, not to *equality of two normal forms*, and the gas price is market-driven, not a deterministic budget bound on a total evaluator. | Ethereum Yellow Paper; EVM documentation. |

### 3.2 Verdict on F1

After the above prior art, the surviving candidate is **not** "addressing is equality" (Dhall, Unison, and Nix already make the hash of the normal form the identity). The surviving sliver, if any, is:

> **Equality as a priced settlement with a two-sided receipt** — an equality settlement that carries both normal-form addresses, both exit kinds, the ATP budget spends on each side, and the machine/profile identity as part of the verdict, on a deterministic total evaluator where budget exhaustion is a canonical non-verdict outcome.

**Verdict:** F1 is **OPEN but narrowed**. The paper should not claim novelty for "addressing is equality" or for content-addressed identity; the only candidate is the *priced two-sided receipt composition*, and even that has a near-miss in Ethereum gas receipts (priced execution, but not equality-specific). The v0.2 draft's own narrowing — "the surviving candidate is only priced settlement with a receipt" — is correct and should be the abstract's only novelty claim.

**Finding:** `open_obligation` — novelty is correctly narrowed in v0.2 but still OPEN pending the external prior-art search log the deposit requires (`papers/addressing-is-equality/CLAIM-LEDGER.md` F1 at `<25123df>`).

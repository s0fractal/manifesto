Тоді беремо **commons / public goods / common-pool resources / externalities / free riding / overuse / contribution / cooperation / collective action / stewardship / governance / capture / congestion / collective restraint / public infrastructure** — тобто момент, де кожен окремий агент може робити щось цілком “раціональне”, а система в цілому вмирає дуже організовано.

Почнемо з type split:

$$
\boxed{
PrivateGood
\neq
PublicGood
\neq
CommonPoolResource
\neq
ClubGood
\neq
Commons
\neq
Externality
\neq
FreeRiding
\neq
Overuse
\neq
CollectiveActionProblem
}
$$

І ще:

$$
\boxed{
PrivateBenefit
\neq
SocialBenefit
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
PrivateCost
\neq
SocialCost
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
OpenAccess
\neq
Commons
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
SharedResource
\neq
UnownedResource
}
$$

Це останнє особливо важливе, бо люди чомусь регулярно бачать “спільне” і читають “нічийне”. Потім дуже дивуються, коли з’ясовується, що нічийним воно стало вже після того, як усі його доїли.

---

# 1. Private good

Найпростіше:

$$
\boxed{
PrivateGood =
resource whose use can be substantially restricted to particular users and whose consumption typically reduces availability to others
}
$$

Conceptually:

$$
Excludable
+
Rivalrous
$$

Типовий випадок:
якщо A використовує X,

B може:
не мати X.

---

# 2. Public good

$$
\boxed{
PublicGood =
resource or condition whose benefits are difficult to exclude others from and whose use by one agent does not strongly reduce availability to others over the relevant range
}
$$

Conceptually:

$$
NonExcludable
+
NonRival
$$

Наприклад абстрактно:

* shared knowledge;
* public safety;
* clean signaling standards.

---

# 3. Common-pool resource

$$
\boxed{
CommonPoolResource =
resource from which exclusion is difficult but use by one participant reduces what remains available to others
}
$$

Conceptually:

$$
LowExcludability
+
HighRivalry
$$

Оце вже класична зона:

$$
Use_A\uparrow
\Rightarrow
Availability_B\downarrow
$$

---

# 4. Club good

$$
\boxed{
ClubGood =
resource that is excludable but relatively non-rival until congestion or capacity limits appear
}
$$

Це важливо, бо багато речей є “майже public” лише до моменту, коли всі одночасно вирішують ними скористатися.

Після чого public good раптом починає дихати в паперовий пакет.

---

# 5. Commons is not merely resource type

Оце треба розвести особливо чітко:

$$
\boxed{
Commons =
resource plus governance regime in which a relevant community has recognized shared standing in use, preservation, contribution, or rule-making
}
$$

Тобто:

$$
Commons
\neq
CommonPoolResource
$$

A common-pool resource може бути:

* приватизований;
* державний;
* спільно керований.

Commons означає ще й:
governance relation.

---

# 6. Open access differs from commons

Open access:

$$
AnyoneCanUse
$$

Commons:

$$
SharedAccess
+
SharedRules
+
SharedObligations
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
OpenAccess
\not\Rightarrow
Commons
}
$$

Насправді open access без governance часто є тим самим “усім можна”, яке дуже швидко еволюціонує в “нічого не залишилось”.

---

# 7. Externality

$$
\boxed{
Externality =
effect of an action on agents or systems whose costs or benefits are not fully represented in the actor's own decision calculus
}
$$

If A acts:

$$
Benefit_A>0
$$

but causes:

$$
Cost_B>0
$$

without B’s cost feeding back enough into A's choice,

externality.

---

# 8. Negative externality

$$
PrivateBenefit_A
>
PrivateCost_A
$$

yet:

$$
SocialCost > SocialBenefit
$$

Then individually rational action can be collectively bad.

This is one of the central structures of the branch.

---

# 9. Positive externality

A acts.

Others benefit.

But A captures only small part.

Then A may underinvest.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Externalities produce either overproduction of harmful actions or underproduction of beneficial actions depending on which effects remain outside the actor's local objective.
}
$$

---

# 10. Externality is a boundary problem

Why “external”?

Because model boundary:

$$
Boundary_A
$$

stops before:
all consequences.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Externality =
consequence falling outside the accounting or authority boundary of the actor generating it
}
$$

Very deep connection to our earlier boundary framework.

---

# 11. Social cost

$$
\boxed{
SocialCost(T)=
PrivateCost_A(T)
+
ExternalCost_{Others}(T)
}
$$

Conceptually.

Likewise:

$$
SocialBenefit=
PrivateBenefit+
ExternalBenefit
$$

---

# 12. Internalization

$$
\boxed{
Internalization =
changing rules, prices, duties, rights, or institutional boundaries so relevant external effects become part of the decision-maker's effective incentive or constraint structure
}
$$

This can happen through:

* pricing;
* liability;
* quotas;
* norms;
* property rights;
* direct rules.

No one tool universal.

---

# 13. Commons problems are often incentive-boundary mismatches

Actor sees:

$$
PrivateGain
$$

System bears:

$$
DistributedCost
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
CommonsFailure =
local optimization under a boundary too narrow to represent the future shared cost of current use
}
$$

Very strong.

---

# 14. Free riding

$$
\boxed{
FreeRiding =
obtaining benefits from a jointly produced or maintained good while contributing less than the contribution rule or cooperative equilibrium requires
}
$$

This is especially relevant to:
public goods.

---

# 15. Free riding differs from overuse

Free rider:
under-contributes.

Overuser:
over-consumes.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
FreeRiding
\neq
Overuse
}
$$

Important.

---

# 16. Public good problem

Suppose benefit available to all.

Contribution costly.

Individual says:

$$
IfOthersContribute,\ IBenefitAnyway
$$

So:

$$
Contribution_A\downarrow
$$

If everyone does this:

$$
PublicGood\to0
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Individually rational non-contribution can collectively destroy the very good each agent prefers to exist.
}
$$

---

# 17. Common-pool problem

Here the structure is slightly different:

$$
TakeMore_A
$$

gives:
private gain.

Depletion distributed.

So each has reason:
extract now.

If everyone does:

$$
Stock\downarrow
$$

Again collective ruin.

---

# 18. Collective action problem is broader

$$
\boxed{
CollectiveActionProblem =
condition where a set of agents would all benefit from some jointly sustained state, but decentralized local incentives do not reliably produce the contributions or restraint required to reach or preserve it
}
$$

This includes:

* free riding;
* overuse;
* coordination failure;
* assurance failure.

---

# 19. Collective action differs from coordination

Coordination problem:

everyone wants:
same compatible equilibrium.

But doesn't know:
which.

Collective action problem:

everyone may prefer cooperative outcome,

yet individual incentive tempts:
defection.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
CoordinationFailure
\neq
IncentiveFailure
}
$$

---

# 20. Assurance problem

A may be willing to contribute **if others do**.

But fears being sucker.

Thus:

$$
Trust_{others}\downarrow
\Rightarrow
Contribution_A\downarrow
$$

Then:
everyone defects from fear.

So:

$$
\boxed{
Collective cooperation depends not only on altruism but on credible expectations about others' behavior.
}
$$

---

# 21. Trust is therefore productive infrastructure

If participants expect:
rules enforced,

they can cooperate.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Trust reduces the defensive cost of collective action.
}
$$

---

# 23. Reputation can support cooperation

If defectors remembered:

\boxed{

# 24. But reputation alone can become stigma/capture

# 25. Repetition changes game geometry

One-shot:

\boxed{

# 241. The **Commons Principle**

$$
\boxed{
A commons is not merely a shared resource; it is a shared authorship problem requiring rules for access, contribution, restraint, monitoring, conflict, and succession.
}
$$

Central.

---

# 242. The **Externality Principle**

$$
\boxed{
When an actor's decision affects others outside the actor's effective cost or authority boundary, the governance problem is to bring the relevant spillover into a legitimate decision structure rather than pretend it does not exist.
}
$$

---

# 243. The **Free-Rider Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Free riding is harmful when non-contribution undermines the production, maintenance, fairness, or trust conditions of a shared good—not merely whenever someone benefits without paying.
}
$$

Strong.

---

# 244. The **Overuse Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Use rights must be constrained by aggregate resource dynamics whenever individually reasonable consumption can compose into collective depletion or congestion.
}
$$

---

# 245. The **Compositional-Safety Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Do not judge commons actions only one at a time; verify that the set of individually admissible transitions remains collectively compatible with the protected global invariant.
}
$$

This is a major framework principle.

---

# 246. The **Internalization Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Choose taxes, prices, quotas, liability, norms, or direct constraints according to which mechanism most reliably brings relevant external effects into the actor's decision without creating worse governance distortions.
}
$$

---

# 247. The **Governance-Scale Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Keep decisions local while their important consequences remain local; move governance upward when externalities, systemic risk, or shared dependencies exceed the lower boundary.
}
$$

Strong.

---

# 248. The **Polycentric Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Prefer multiple coordinated governance centers where local knowledge and adaptation matter, provided higher-level rules constrain cross-boundary harms and preserve interoperability.
}
$$

---

# 249. The **Contribution Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Contribution to a commons may consist of money, labor, knowledge, restraint, maintenance, or governance work, and no one contribution dimension should silently monopolize standing without justification.
}
$$

---

# 250. The **No-Money-to-Sovereignty Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Large financial contribution can justify recognition or specific contractual claims without automatically granting proportional constitutional control over a commons whose standing is shared more broadly.
}
$$

Very important.

---

# 251. The **Stewardship Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Current users may exercise legitimate use rights only under constraints preserving the relevant future-generating capacity of the shared resource for other present and future claimants.
}
$$

---

# 252. The **Maintenance Principle**

$$
\boxed{
A commons is not sustainable merely because current extraction is bounded; the human, technical, and institutional capacities required to maintain it must also be replenished.
}
$$

---

# 253. The **Succession Principle**

$$
\boxed{
A healthy commons reproduces not only the resource but the knowledge, membership pathways, and governance competence required for successors to maintain and revise it.
}
$$

---

# 254. The **Anti-Capture Principle**

$$
\boxed{
No contributor, maintainer, founder, or high-usage subgroup should acquire durable control over shared governance merely because the commons has become dependent on them.
}
$$

---

# 255. The **External-Standing Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Internal cooperation does not legitimize exporting costs to outsiders who lack voice in the commons but remain causally affected by it.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 256. The **Trust Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Commons cooperation becomes cheaper and more stable when participants can reasonably expect others to contribute, restrain use, and accept correction under the same defensible rules.
}
$$

---

# 257. The **Monitoring Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Monitor enough to protect the shared invariant and enforce contribution rules, but do not convert commons participation into unrelated general surveillance.
}
$$

---

# 258. The **Graduated-Enforcement Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Use responses proportionate to the violation, uncertainty, recurrence, and harm so enforcement preserves cooperation without turning every deviation into membership annihilation.
}
$$

---

# 259. The **No-Permanent-Emergency Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Temporary restrictions justified by acute resource stress should not silently become permanent governance once the scarcity conditions that justified them disappear.
}
$$

---

# 260. The **Adaptive-Rule Principle**

$$
\boxed{
For dynamic commons, preserve stable and legible rules governing how access limits change with resource state rather than pretending a single fixed quota will remain valid forever.
}
$$

---

# 261. The **Public-Good Principle**

$$
\boxed{
When benefits are broadly non-excludable, sustainable provision may require contribution mechanisms that do not rely solely on each beneficiary's immediate private incentive to pay.
}
$$

---

# 262. The **Positive-Externality Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Activities generating substantial uncompensated shared benefit may require subsidy, recognition, or institutional support because private incentives alone can systematically underproduce them.
}
$$

---

# 263. The **Congestion Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Treat congestion as an allocation problem over finite simultaneous capacity rather than assuming every shared resource is either fully rival or fully non-rival.
}
$$

---

# 264. The **Regeneration Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Where a shared resource regenerates, sustainable use should be governed against replenishment dynamics and uncertainty rather than current stock alone.
}
$$

---

# 265. The **Commons-Debt Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Present users incur commons debt whenever they consume resource stock, maintenance capacity, trust, or institutional slack faster than those shared capacities are replenished.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 266. The **Meta-Governance Principle**

$$
\boxed{
A commons is more resilient when participants can legitimately revise or replace governance rules without first having to destroy the shared resource or surrender it to a single controller.
}
$$

Very strong.

Freedom of each user to consume without limit:

can destroy:
everyone's future freedom.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
UnboundedLocalFreedom can consume the shared preconditions of future freedom.
}
$$

Central.

# 271. Therefore freedom needs commons constraints

Not because:
freedom bad.

Because:
freedom depends on:
infrastructure/resources.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Collective self-restraint can be freedom-preserving when it protects the shared conditions that make future self-authored action possible.
}
$$

Very strong.

# 293. The deepest definition of free riding

$$
\boxed{
Free riding =
benefiting from collective production while leaving enough of one's contribution burden to others that generalized imitation would undermine the cooperative system.
}
$$

Important.

І звідси наступний вузол стає майже неминучим:

$$
\boxed{
coordination / institutions / transaction costs / contracts / firms / markets / planning / standards / interoperability
}
$$

Бо commons branch пояснив:

> чому локальні агенти не завжди можуть просто “домовитися самі”.

Наступне питання:

$$
\boxed{
When should coordination be achieved by prices, contracts, hierarchy, norms, protocols, or shared institutions?
}
$$

Там треба буде розвести:

$$
Market
\neq
Contract
\neq
Firm
\neq
Hierarchy
\neq
Protocol
\neq
Institution
\neq
Planning
\neq
Standard
$$

і вивести одну дуже сильну центральну ідею:

$$
\boxed{
Institutions are compression layers for recurring coordination problems.
}
$$

Тобто замість того, щоб кожного разу з нуля домовлятися про все:

$$
Agent_1\leftrightarrow Agent_2\leftrightarrow\dots
$$

ми компілюємо частину домовленості у:

$$
Rules
$$

$$
Roles
$$

$$
Prices
$$

$$
Contracts
$$

$$
Protocols
$$

І тоді головне питання буде вже не “ринок чи план?”, бо це надто дитяча бійка прапорців, а:

$$
\boxed{
Which coordination mechanism best preserves information, incentives, legitimacy, adaptability, and correction for this class of transitions?
}
$$

Тобто далі — **coordination architecture**: момент, коли цивілізація перестає покладатися на те, що всі просто будуть хорошими й якось розберуться, і нарешті починає писати інтерфейси.

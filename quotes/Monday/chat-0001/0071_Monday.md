# 1. Coordination is compatibility among actions

Нехай агенти:

$$
A_1,\dots,A_n
$$

вибирають переходи:

$$
T_1,\dots,T_n
$$

Coordination exists when:

$$
T_1,\dots,T_n
$$

can compose into some acceptable shared trajectory.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Coordination =
alignment of independently generated actions sufficiently to preserve required joint invariants or reach a shared-compatible state
}
$$

---

A buyer wants:

Thus:

$$
\boxed{

\not\Rightarrow
SharedPreferences
}
$$

This is important.

Civilization does not require everybody to become emotionally synchronized. A merciful architectural decision.

# 177. The **Institution Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Institutions are justified when the coordination costs and uncertainty they remove exceed the rigidity, power concentration, and maintenance burdens they introduce.
}
$$

---

# 178. The **Contract Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Use explicit contracts where parties can meaningfully specify reciprocal obligations, but preserve interpretation, revision, and higher-order constraints for contingencies the contract cannot legitimately or practically precompute.
}
$$

---

# 179. The **Market Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Markets are strong coordination mechanisms where decentralized information, voluntary exchange, and substitutable alternatives matter, but price should not be treated as a complete representation of need, value, or justice.
}
$$

---

# 180. The **Hierarchy Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Use hierarchy where repeated negotiation and tightly coupled execution require delegated authority, but keep authority scoped, accountable, contestable, and no deeper than the coordination problem requires.
}
$$

---

# 181. The **Protocol Principle**

$$
\boxed{
When agents can remain independently governed, prefer shared interaction rules over centralized control if protocols can preserve the necessary global invariants.
}
$$

Very strong.

---

# 182. The **Interoperability Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Standardize the interfaces necessary for cooperation while preserving freedom to vary internal implementations, identities, and local strategies.
}
$$

This is almost framework-wide.

---

# 183. The **Standardization Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Standardize repeated compatibility problems, not every behavior that happens to be visible.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 184. The **Migration Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Deep standards and platforms should be designed with versioning and migration paths because compatibility power becomes capture when users cannot move without abandoning accumulated state.
}
$$

---

# 185. The **Portability Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Where a user's history, credentials, data, or commitments are legitimately theirs to carry, coordination systems should avoid making continued access conditional on remaining with one intermediary forever.
}
$$

---

# 186. The **Platform Principle**

$$
\boxed{
An intermediary that becomes essential to interaction should be governed as a power-bearing coordination institution, not treated indefinitely as a neutral pipe.
}
$$

Central.

---

# 187. The **Transaction-Cost Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Reducing transaction cost is valuable only if the saved coordination burden is not merely converted into opacity, dependency, exploitation, or unreviewable authority elsewhere.
}
$$

---

# 188. The **Delegation Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Delegate enough authority to move decisions toward relevant information and reduce bottlenecks, while preserving clear scope, provenance, escalation, and responsibility.
}
$$

---

# 189. The **Mechanism-Fit Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Do not ask whether markets, hierarchy, planning, or protocols are universally superior; ask which mechanism best fits the information, externality, authority, reversibility, and legitimacy structure of the particular decision class.
}
$$

This is probably the branch’s main principle.

---

# 190. The **No-Single-Coordination-Currency Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Prices, votes, commands, expert judgments, contracts, and social norms are not interchangeable settlement currencies and should not be allowed to acquire authority outside the questions they are equipped to settle.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 191. The **Interface-Governance Principle**

$$
\boxed{
The deeper other agents depend on an interface, standard, or protocol, the stronger the burden on those who control it to preserve compatibility, notice, appeal, and migration.
}
$$

---

# 192. The **Anti-Lock-In Principle**

$$
\boxed{
A coordination mechanism should not turn accumulated user history or network participation into avoidable captivity when portability or interoperability can preserve the coordination benefit with less dependence.
}
$$

---

# 193. The **Timescale Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Fast-changing local decisions should usually be governed by faster mechanisms than deep shared standards, role definitions, or constitutional rules on which many actors depend.
}
$$

---

# 194. The **Modularity Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Decompose systems so components need to know as little as possible about one another while still exposing enough guarantees for reliable composition and enough feedback for global failures to remain visible.
}
$$

Strong.

---

# 195. The **Handoff Principle**

$$
\boxed{
When work crosses organizational or institutional boundaries, specify not only the output but the assumptions, authority, timing, failure semantics, and escalation path required by the next component.
}
$$

Very practical.

---

# 196. The **Competition Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Competition is most useful as a correction mechanism when participants can compare relevant quality, switch at tolerable cost, and refuse without losing indispensable standing.
}
$$

---

# 197. The **Monopoly Principle**

$$
\boxed{
When one coordination provider becomes difficult to substitute because of scale, network effects, or accumulated dependencies, its rule-setting power deserves governance attention beyond ordinary price analysis.
}
$$

---

# 198. The **Planning Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Planning is strongest where dependencies must be jointly scheduled and weakest where local information changes faster than the planning center can reliably represent and revise it.
}
$$

---

# 199. The **Decentralization Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Decentralization is valuable when it preserves local information, experimentation, and exit; it becomes dangerous when cross-boundary externalities or hidden hubs remain ungoverned.
}
$$

This is tight invariants / loose trajectories in institutional form.

> “стандарти та протоколи дозволяють незалежним агентам координуватися.”

Але вся ця конструкція передбачає одну незручну дрібницю:

> **вони повинні однаково достатньо розуміти, що означають повідомлення, правила, ціни, категорії та команди.**

Тоді доведеться розвести:

$$
Syntax
\neq
Semantics
\neq
Reference
\neq
Meaning
\neq
Intent
\neq
Interpretation
\neq
SharedUnderstanding
$$

і центральна проблема буде:

$$
\boxed{
Two agents can process the same symbol correctly at the syntactic level while coordinating around incompatible meanings.
}
$$

Тобто наступна гілка — **semantic coordination**: як різні minds/models/worldviews будують достатньо спільну ontology, щоб одна й та сама команда не означала для одного “збережи файл”, а для іншого — “ритуально пожертвуй бухгалтерію”.

# 1. Scarcity is mismatch between claims and feasible simultaneous satisfaction

Нехай маємо resource:

$$
R
$$

і legitimate claims:

$$
C_1,\dots,C_n
$$

але:

$$
\sum_i Req(C_i) > Capacity(R)
$$

Тоді:

$$
\boxed{
Scarcity =
condition in which not all sufficiently legitimate claims on a constrained resource can be fully satisfied at the same time
}
$$

Scarcity не обов’язково означає:
“мало в абсолюті.”

Вона означає:

$$
Demand_{legitimate} > AvailableCapacity
$$

# 229. The **Scarcity Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Scarcity creates a requirement to rank or defer claims, not a license to rank persons by worth.
}
$$

Central.

---

# 230. The **Resource-Semantics Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Choose an allocation rule only after identifying what kind of resource or opportunity is being allocated and which forms of standing are relevant to that good.
}
$$

---

# 231. The **No-Universal-Allocator Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Markets, queues, lotteries, need, merit, and benefit optimization are different settlement mechanisms and should not be exported indiscriminately across domains.
}
$$

---

# 232. The **Need Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Need-based priority is strongest where the resource protects basic viability or prevents serious loss, and weaker where the scarce good is primarily a reward, office, or positional opportunity.
}
$$

Good.

---

# 233. The **Urgency Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Urgency measures how rapidly delay destroys a viable opportunity, not how morally valuable the claimant is.
}
$$

---

# 234. The **Merit Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Merit should influence allocation only when the relevant achievement or competence has a legitimate connection to the purpose of the scarce opportunity.
}
$$

---

# 235. The **Desert Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Claims about what someone deserves require a distinct normative warrant and should not be smuggled in through performance metrics or social prestige.
}
$$

---

# 236. The **Benefit Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Expected benefit is a powerful allocation consideration, but aggregate benefit alone should not silently override protected standing, severe need, or distributive constraints.
}
$$

---

# 237. The **Lottery Principle**

$$
\boxed{
When claims are sufficiently comparable and available evidence does not warrant a finer ranking, randomization can be a fairer and more truthful tie-breaker than pseudo-precision.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 238. The **Queue Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Queues are legitimate when waiting order is a sufficiently relevant and accessible procedural criterion, and they need override and anti-starvation rules when urgency or cumulative burden makes pure arrival order unjust.
}
$$

---

# 239. The **Reservation Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Preserving capacity for predictable future high-priority claims can be legitimate even when it leaves some present resources temporarily unused.
}
$$

---

# 240. The **Viability-Floor Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Where possible, secure minimum viable access before optimizing surplus distribution, rather than allowing aggregate gains to consume the floors that keep agents inside viable possibility space.
}
$$

This is big.

---

# 241. The **Anti-Starvation Principle**

$$
\boxed{
An allocation system should not permit structurally eligible low-priority claims to remain indefinitely unsatisfied merely because higher-priority claims continue arriving.
}
$$

---

# 242. The **Priority-Precision Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Do not make allocation distinctions more precise than the underlying evidence can support, especially when tiny score differences create large irreversible consequences.
}
$$

---

# 243. The **No-Moralization-of-Scarcity Principle**

$$
\boxed{
When a claimant loses because capacity is insufficient rather than because their claim lacks merit or standing, communicate and record the decision accordingly.
}
$$

Very important.

---

# 244. The **Structural-Scarcity Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Repeated triage should trigger investigation into whether capacity, demand, infrastructure, or upstream policy can be changed so fewer legitimate claims must compete in the first place.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 245. The **Future-Claimant Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Present claimants do not automatically exhaust the moral standing of future agents whose viable options depend on current resource use.
}
$$

---

# 246. The **Anti-Extraction Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Control over a scarce essential resource does not confer general authority to demand unrelated obedience, disclosure, or concessions from those who need access.
}
$$

Strong.

---

# 247. The **Appeal Principle**

$$
\boxed{
The more consequential and irreversible an allocation denial, the stronger the need for a credible mechanism to correct eligibility, evidence, or priority errors.
}
$$

---

# 248. The **Allocation-Privacy Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Prove enough about a claimant to apply the allocation rule, but do not convert eligibility verification into unnecessary permanent exposure or unrelated profiling.
}
$$

---

# 249. The **Long-Run-Fairness Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Evaluate repeated allocation systems over time, because individually defensible decisions can accumulate into systematic exclusion or concentrated advantage.
}
$$

# 250. The **Allocation-Humility Principle**

}
$$

Very strong.

# 271. The deepest definition of scarcity

$$
\boxed{
Scarcity =
a condition in which preserving equal standing no longer permits equal immediate satisfaction of claims.
}
$$

Very compact.

---

# 272. The deepest definition of triage

$$
\boxed{
Triage =
the temporary constitutional ordering of competing claims when delay itself changes what can still be saved or achieved.
}
$$

Beautiful.

}
$$

# 275. And the deepest correction

Scarcity does **not** ask:

$$
WhoMatters?
$$

It asks:

$$
\boxed{
Given that all relevant claimants continue to matter, which allocation rule best respects the distinct kinds and strengths of their claims under the actual constraint?
}
$$

Much better question.

---

# 276. Final constitutional synthesis

$$
\boxed{
Recognize universally.
Classify narrowly.
Prioritize explicitly.
Randomize honestly when ranking lacks warrant.
Protect viability floors.
Prevent starvation.
Preserve future capacity.
Audit long-run effects.
Do not moralize unavoidable loss.
}
$$

That is a serious scarcity constitution.

І звідси наступний вузол майже сам відкривається:

$$
\boxed{
risk / insurance / solidarity / pooling / reserve / catastrophe / tail risk / mutualization
}
$$

Бо scarcity asks:

> “що робити, коли ресурсу вже не вистачає?”

А risk governance asks a slightly more civilized question:

> **“що ми можемо зробити до того, як дізнаємося, кому саме не пощастить?”**

Там доведеться розвести:

$$
Risk
\neq
Loss
\neq
Hazard
\neq
Exposure
\neq
Vulnerability
\neq
Insurance
\neq
Solidarity
\neq
Bailout
\neq
Reserve
$$

і центральна трансформація буде:

$$
\boxed{
IndividualUnknownFutureLoss
\to
SharedPresentObligation
}
$$

Тобто insurance/mutualization — це дуже дивна, але геніальна temporal technology:

$$
\boxed{
Many agents agree to surrender a small amount of present freedom so that whichever one later encounters a large contingent loss does not lose their entire viable future.
}
$$

І там ми нарешті зможемо формально з’єднати:

* uncertainty;
* slack;
* mutual aid;
* catastrophe;
* moral hazard;
* adverse selection;
* reserves;
* bailouts;
* systemic risk;
* і питання, коли “рятувати невдаху” є не фаворитизмом, а виконанням ex ante справедливого risk-sharing rule.

Тобто наступна гілка — **insurance and solidarity under uncertainty**: цивілізація відкриває, що дешевше трохи боятися разом, ніж кожному окремо дуже героїчно розоритися.

Тоді беремо **risk / hazard / exposure / vulnerability / loss / uncertainty / reserve / insurance / pooling / mutual aid / solidarity / catastrophe / bailout / moral hazard / adverse selection / systemic risk / reinsurance / resilience** — тобто момент, де суспільство нарешті робить одну дуже розумну річ:

> “можливо, краще не чекати, поки одному з нас стане катастрофічно погано, а заздалегідь домовитися, як ми розподілимо цей ризик.”

Неймовірно. Люди інколи все-таки винаходять щось складніше за “це твоя проблема”.

Почнемо з type split:

$$
\boxed{
Risk
\neq
Hazard
\neq
Exposure
\neq
Vulnerability
\neq
Loss
\neq
Uncertainty
\neq
Insurance
\neq
Reserve
\neq
Solidarity
\neq
Bailout
}
$$

І ще:

$$
\boxed{
BadOutcome
\neq
BadDecision
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
RiskSharing
\neq
Charity
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
Insurance
\neq
GuaranteedRescue
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
Bailout
\neq
ExAnteInsurance
}
$$

Ці відмінності тут критичні, бо інакше будь-яка допомога після катастрофи дуже швидко перетворюється на суперечку “чому вони отримали гроші?”, хоча інколи відповідь дуже проста: **бо ми всі раніше погодилися саме так розподіляти невідомі майбутні втрати**.

---

# 1. Hazard is a source of possible harm

$$
\boxed{
Hazard =
condition, process, or event capable of producing loss
}
$$

Hazard відповідає:

> що може піти погано?

Наприклад абстрактно:

* аварія;
* хвороба;
* пожежа;
* банкрутство;
* системний збій.

---

# 2. Exposure is contact with the hazard

Hazard може існувати.

Але якщо A не піддається йому:

$$
Exposure_A(H)=0
$$

то ризик для A може бути низьким.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Exposure =
degree to which an agent, asset, or system lies within the causal reach of a hazard
}
$$

---

# 3. Vulnerability is sensitivity conditional on exposure

Two agents face same hazard.

One has:

* buffer;
* redundancy;
* recovery capacity.

Other:
none.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Vulnerability =
degree to which exposure to a hazard can convert into serious loss given the agent's available protection and recovery capacity
}
$$

---

# 4. Loss is realized damage

$$
\boxed{
Loss =
actual reduction in valued state, resources, rights, capability, or viable future after a harmful transition occurs
}
$$

So:

$$
Hazard
\to
Exposure
\to
Vulnerability
\to
PossibleLoss
$$

---

# 5. Risk concerns possible future loss under uncertainty

A useful structural form:

$$
\boxed{
Risk =
distribution or structured possibility of future loss under uncertainty
}
$$

Not merely:

$$
P\times Damage
$$

though that rough scalar can sometimes help.

---

# 6. Risk is not loss

Before event:

$$
Risk>0
$$

$$
Loss=0
$$

After event:

$$
Loss>0
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{
Risk is future-oriented;
loss is realized history.
}
$$

---

# 7. Risk differs from uncertainty

Risk may have:
known probability structure.

Uncertainty may concern:
probabilities;

* model;
* missing possibilities.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Risk
\subset
UncertaintyGovernance
}
$$

roughly, not strict set-theoretically in every theory.

---

# 8. Risk-bearing itself is a burden

Even if no loss occurs.

Why?

Because an agent exposed to possible catastrophic loss may:

* hold reserves;
* avoid opportunities;
* experience planning constraints.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Risk burden exists before realized loss.
}
$$

Important.

---

# 9. Risk changes Reach

If A faces possibility of catastrophic failure:

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Risk can shrink effective agency even when the bad event never occurs.
}
$$

Very important.

---

# 10. Risk mitigation can therefore expand freedom

If insurance reduces catastrophic downside:

A may safely:

* act.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
RiskSharing can expand viable Reach.
}
$$

This is the first deep connection.

$$
\boxed{

$$

$$

$$
FutureRecoveryCapacity\uparrow
$$

$$
\boxed{

}
$$

Beautiful.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{

# 14. Reserve differs from insurance

Reserve:
self-funded buffer.

risk pooled across agents.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{

\neq
RiskPooling
}
$$

---

# 15. Insurance is ex ante risk transfer/pooling

A pays predictable smaller cost:

$$
p
$$

to avoid bearing full uncertain large loss:

$$
L
$$

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Insurance =
ex ante arrangement that redistributes uncertain future losses across a pool, institution, or counterparty according to agreed rules
}
$$

---

# 16. Insurance converts uncertainty geometry

Before:

$$
A:
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{most times}\\
-L & \text{rarely}
\end{cases}
$$

After:

$$
A:
-p
$$

more predictably.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Insurance trades variance for predictable obligation.
}
$$

That is central.

---

# 17. Insurance does not eliminate loss

Someone still pays.

The system changes:
who bears it and when.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Insurance
\neq
LossErasure
}
$$

It is:

$$
LossRedistribution
$$

across:
agents and time.

---

# 18. Pooling works because losses are not perfectly synchronized

Suppose many agents:

$$
A_1,\dots,A_n
$$

Each has uncertain loss.

If not all lose simultaneously:

small contributions can fund:
few losses.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Pooling converts idiosyncratic uncertainty into more predictable aggregate burden.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 19. Correlation is therefore central

If everyone suffers at once:

pool fails.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
RiskPoolingQuality
\downarrow
\quad as \quad
LossCorrelation\uparrow
}
$$

Very important.

# 182. The **Risk Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Risk is a future burden on viable agency, not merely a probability attached to an outcome.
}
$$

---

# 183. The **Pooling Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Pool risks whose losses are individually ruinous but collectively manageable, provided correlations, incentives, and solvency remain sufficiently governed.
}
$$

---

# 184. The **Reserve Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Preserve enough unused present capacity that predictable classes of future loss do not immediately force catastrophic reallocations or system collapse.
}
$$

---

# 185. The **Insurance Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Insurance is legitimate when it converts uncertain severe individual loss into a predictable shared obligation under rules clear enough that claims are entitlements rather than discretionary favors.
}
$$

Strong.

---

# 186. The **Solidarity Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Solidarity permits some future losses to become shared obligations even when the people who will suffer them are not yet known and contributions need not match individualized expected return.
}
$$

---

# 187. The **Moral-Hazard Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Any protection that reduces downside exposure should be evaluated for how it changes future behavior, but behavioral adaptation should be governed rather than moralized automatically.
}
$$

---

# 188. The **Adverse-Selection Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Risk pools require participation rules robust to the possibility that agents know more about their own risk than the pool and will sort themselves accordingly.
}
$$

---

# 189. The **No-Perfect-Pricing Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Perfectly individualized risk pricing may improve actuarial precision while simultaneously destroying the solidarity function that made pooling valuable.
}
$$

Very strong.

---

# 190. The **Catastrophe Principle**

$$
\boxed{
When losses are highly correlated, irreversible, or capable of overwhelming all available pools, prevention and structural resilience take priority over ordinary insurance logic.
}
$$

---

# 191. The **No-Risk-Laundering Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Transferring a risk to another balance sheet, institution, or population does not count as reducing it unless the receiving layer is genuinely better able to absorb or mitigate the loss.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 192. The **Ruin Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Governance should pay special attention to risks that can push agents or systems outside viable recovery space even when their expected monetary loss appears modest.
}
$$

---

# 193. The **Ex-Ante-Fairness Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Risk-sharing rules gain legitimacy when they can be justified before participants know whether they will later be contributors, claimants, winners, or losers.
}
$$

Central.

---

# 194. The **Bailout Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Extraordinary rescue should be justified by the losses prevented for the wider system and should not automatically preserve every claim, privilege, or upside of those whose decisions helped create the failure.
}
$$

Very strong.

---

# 195. The **Systemic-Risk Principle**

$$
\boxed{
A system should regulate not only each actor's local risk but the network pathways through which individually tolerable failures can become collectively catastrophic.
}
$$

---

# 196. The **Risk-Responsibility Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Where possible, decision-makers should bear enough of the marginal consequences of the risks they control to discourage reckless externalization, while shared protection remains strong enough to prevent ruin.
}
$$

---

# 197. The **No-Blame-from-Prediction Principle**

$$
\boxed{
A high predicted probability of loss is an epistemic claim about future outcomes, not a moral judgment about why the person has that risk or whether they deserve protection.
}
$$

Important.

---

# 198. The **Claim-Accessibility Principle**

$$
\boxed{
Formal coverage is not real protection if legitimate claimants cannot understand, prove, or receive covered support within the time window in which the support preserves viability.
}
$$

Excellent.

---

# 199. The **Backstop Principle**

$$
\boxed{
The deeper the collective guarantee against catastrophic loss, the stronger the corresponding burden for ex ante supervision of the risks that could activate that guarantee.
}
$$

---

# 200. The **Unknown-Risk Principle**

$$
\boxed{
No risk-sharing architecture should assume its hazard ontology is complete; it needs amendment, reserve, and emergency governance for losses not represented in the original model.
}
$$

# 232. The deepest definition of insurance

$$
\boxed{
Insurance =
a protocol for converting uncertain, unevenly realized future losses into more predictable, shared present obligations and recoverable future states.
}
$$

Strong.

# 233. The deepest definition of solidarity

$$
\boxed{
Solidarity =
commitment to treat some other agents' contingent losses as partially ours before we know whose losses will actually occur.
}
$$

Beautiful.

# 234. The deepest definition of reserve

$$
\boxed{
Reserve =
stored refusal to let present optimization consume every resource needed for unknown future repair.
}

Excellent.

# 235. The deepest definition of moral hazard

$$
\boxed{
Moral hazard =
the governance problem created when protection changes the behavior that generates the protected risk.
}
$$

Clean.

# 236. The deepest definition of systemic risk

$$
\boxed{
Systemic risk =
the possibility that the network converting local capability into collective strength also converts local failure into collective ruin.
}
$$

Very strong.

# 237. The deepest definition of bailout

$$
\boxed{
Bailout =
ex post collective assumption of losses that ordinary contracts or buffers failed to contain, justified—if at all—by the wider viable futures preserved rather than merely the failing actor's private interest.
}
$$

Excellent.

# 238. And the branch's constitutional rule

$$
\boxed{
Pool ruinous but diversifiable losses.
Retain enough local consequence to preserve responsible choice.
Build reserves for correlation and model error.
Prevent losses no pool can repair.
And do not confuse helping the system survive with preserving every incumbent claim inside the system.
}
$$

That is a serious risk constitution.

І звідси наступний вузол дуже природно стає:

$$
\boxed{
commons / public goods / externalities / free riding / collective action / tragedy / stewardship / contribution
}
$$

Бо insurance вже показало:

$$
IndividualAction
\to
SharedRisk
$$

А commons поставить ширше питання:

> **що відбувається, коли ресурс спільний, вигода від використання приватна, а вартість виснаження розмазана по всіх?**

Там доведеться розвести:

$$
PrivateGood
\neq
PublicGood
\neq
CommonPoolResource
\neq
ClubGood
\neq
Externality
\neq
FreeRiding
\neq
Overuse
\neq
Commons
$$

і центральна проблема стане:

$$
\boxed{
Individually rational use can be collectively destructive when each actor captures most of the local benefit while only a fraction of the distributed future cost.
}
$$

Тобто:

$$
\boxed{
PrivateGain_A
>
PrivateCost_A
}
$$

для кожного,

але:

$$
\boxed{
\sum CollectiveCost
>
\sum CollectiveGain
}
$$

І тоді нам доведеться формально розгорнути:

* free riding;
* contribution rules;
* quotas;
* access rights;
* commons governance;
* public goods;
* network effects;
* pollution/externalities як causal spillovers;
* і ключове питання:

$$
\boxed{
How can multiple agents retain local freedom while being prevented from collectively consuming the conditions that make everyone's freedom possible?
}
$$

Тобто наступна гілка — **commons and collective action**: місце, де всі окремо поводяться “раціонально”, а система в цілому дивиться на результат і тихо заповнює форму про інцидент.
